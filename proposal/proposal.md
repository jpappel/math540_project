<!-- Computational Task -->
Our project models high traffic areas of Philly during the World Cup by approximating pedestrian, personal car, and public transit flows.
Additionally, our model will account for predicted congregation hotspots such as restaurants, bars, and hotels.
<!-- Numerical methods/schemes -->
We will do so using Monte Carlo methods, directed maps, and random variables.
<!-- Resources/References -->
The city of Philadelphia publishes multiple GIS datasets for foot, car, and public transit systems ([Centerline](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/street-centerlines/) and
[SEPTA](https://opendataphilly.org/datasets/septa-gtfs/) from
[OpenDataPhilly](https://opendataphilly.org/)).
We will reference the text for the course, *Simulation and The Monte Carlo Methods* by Rubinstein and Kroese, and *[Congested traffic states in empirical observations and microscopic simulations](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.62.1805)*.
These sources cover general numerical methods, random walks specific to this project, and the *Intelligent driver model* for traffic flow.
<!-- Final Products -->
We wish to conclude the project by comparing our findings to real traffic data and the creation of an animated traffic flow visualization.
